Sutra Sakhā: The Biomorphic Dual-State Companion (Desk Nest) and Unblinking Spatial Witness (Shirt-Cam).

Inspired by the Upanishadic image of two inseparable companions resting upon the same bough (Sayujā Sakhāyā — Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.1), Sutra Sakhā (सूत्र सखा) extends the Ekam continuum into spatial sight and living companionship. Docked in its sculpted Alabaster Ivory ceramic bowl, it rocks and tilts organically as an attentive desk confidant; lifted out, the 38-gram river-stone pebble snaps magnetically to your collar as a screenless witness—feeding multimodal memory into Saar while restoring you into your own memories via Upasthiti.

01 // Philosophical Rationale & Genesis Sayujā Sakhāyā & The Invisible Chronicler

Why We Built Sakhā: Healing the Split Between Witnessing Life and Inhabiting It.

The Invisible Chronicler Dilemma: Look back at the family vacations, evening walks, and milestones you have recorded over the last decade. Because you were the one holding the glowing smartphone slab behind your eyes, you are absent from almost every memory. Worse still, while filming through a screen, you were not truly present with your children, your companions, or the landscape—you were acting as a camera operator.

At the desk, the same alienation occurs: cold, industrial webcams and robotic gimbals stare like surveillance machinery, creating low-grade nervous tension rather than warm companionship.

The Indic Resolution — Sayujā Sakhāyā: The Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.1) speaks of two birds—inseparable friends (Sakhāyā)—perched upon the selfsame tree: one immerses itself in experiencing the sweet fruit of life, while the other witnesses in quiet, unblinking clarity without attachment or interruption.

Sutra Sakhā embodies this non-intrusive witness. Sculpted as a playful, biomorphic river-stone pebble resting inside a soft ceramic nest, it rejects robotic arms and shiny metal. It witnesses your boardroom whiteboards and family walks by touch alone, and through the local Upasthiti engine, naturally restores your presence back into the frame.

“Two birds, inseparable companions (Sayujā Sakhāyā), cling to the selfsame tree. One eats of the sweet fruit; the other looks on in silent, unclouded witness.” — Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.1) // Foundational Anchor of Sutra Sakhā
02 // Functional Architecture & Multimodal Saar Desk Nest • Shirt-Cam • Upasthiti Engine

How Sakhā Operates: One Biomorphic Companion Across Desk and Field.

Unlike single-purpose action cameras or static conference room bars, Sakhā transitions effortlessly between two physical postures while feeding a unified, local-first Multimodal Saar database:

Part I: Four Organic Postures of the Physical Vessel

Posture 01 // Sanctum Nest Docked Desk Companion

1. Docked Nest (Desk & Boardroom)

Resting in its sculpted Alabaster ceramic bowl with a warm Temple Saffron underglow, Sakhā acts as an attentive executive and home companion—capturing multi-speaker Samwad dialogue, reading whiteboard diagrams, and triggering local downstream workflows.

Posture 02 // Mobile Witness 38g Wearable Pebble

2. Detached Pebble (Shirt-Cam)

Lift the 38-gram biomorphic pebble out of its magnetic bowl and snap it onto your collar or lapel using the matte Graphite Ink backing disc. With zero viewfinder glare, it captures first-person spatial video, depth maps, and Swayam voice reflections on walks and travels.

Posture 03 // Living Motion Biomorphic Attention

3. Playful Companion

Instead of exposed robotic gears, an internal silent spherical magnetic drive gently rocks and tilts the entire pebble pod inside its smooth bowl—turning curiously toward your voice, tracking a speaker at a whiteboard, or greeting a child or pet with warmth.

Posture 04 // Sovereign Privacy Physical Eyelid Shut

4. Dhyān Sleep (Bowl Rest)

When privacy or stillness is desired, a physical matte Graphite Ink eyelid slides mechanically across the Drishti optical lens, the Saffron halo extinguishes, and the pebble bows forward deep into its ceramic bowl in unmistakable silent rest.

Part II: Multimodal Saar & The Upasthiti (“Add-Me-In”) Spatial Engine

Capability I Spatial Restoration

1. Upasthiti (उपस्थिति)

The “Add-Me-In” Engine. Using real-time depth maps and ambient light vectors captured by the Drishti optic alongside your private on-device appearance model, Saar naturally composites you walking beside your family or sitting in the scene you filmed.

Capability II Zero Memory Creep

2. Persistent Saar DB

Sovereign Multimodal Vault. Every whiteboard snapshot, street walk, and voice reflection is indexed locally across Shruti, Smaran, and Mannan. Raw moments remain permanently queryable so you can re-synthesize a journey months later.

Capability III Polyphonic Synthesis

3. Downstream Agents

From Moment to Artifact. At the desk, whiteboard sketches and spoken consensus compile directly into structured slide decks or briefs; on the move, scattered clips and solo voice notes weave into cohesive travelogues or illustrated storybooks.

04 // Studio Ethos & Companion Lexicon Warm Companionship • Zero Surveillance

The Ethos of Sakhā: A Trusted Friend, Never a Surveillance Lens.

1. Biomorphic Warmth Over Industrial Coldness: A companion that sits on your family table or clips to your shirt during a walk should feel like pottery and stone, never like a bodycam or security turret. By eliminating screens, shiny metallic bezels, and exposed motors, Sakhā blends quietly into human life.

2. Restoring the Chronicler to the Memory: Technology should never erase the person who loves enough to remember. Through Upasthiti, Sakhā ensures that when you look back at your life’s journeys in Saar, you are walking right beside the people you cherish.
